Valuation Toolkit · 5 companies · 2 markets

A valuation toolkit

Excel3-Statement DCFBank Excess-Return

Different businesses need different maths. The four US companies use a discounted cash flow model: forecast the cash the business will produce, then work out what that cash is worth today. Bank Mandiri uses a different method, because for a bank, borrowed money is the raw material rather than the funding, so the usual cash-flow approach does not apply. Apple is the worked example below.

Inside the model

  • The three statements are linked, so the balance sheet has to balance in every period and a check row says so. The engine was also tested against a published case study and reproduced its answer to the exact dollar
  • The discount rate is worked out, not guessed. Start with the government bond yield, subtract that country’s own default risk, then add a risk premium and the company’s beta. A check row confirms the rate the model uses matches the one just built, tying out to 0.003%
  • The forecasts are scored against reported results. In the first scored period all five models came in low. Apple’s revenue by 9.6%, EBIT by 13.8%. Those fair values are marked superseded on the sheets until rebuilt
  • The cause was a straight-line growth assumption with no real event in it. A guard now blocks that shape before a model can ship
  • The things that have to go right are written as percentages, not adjectives: buybacks staying above $100bn a year at 85%, Services growth holding at or above 12% at 70%
  • Any figure not taken straight from a filing is marked in the sheet as an estimate

Ownership & Broker-Flow Analysis · 5 stocks

Ownership & broker-flow analysis, shown here on PT Barito Pacific (IDX: BRPT)

PythonIDX Broker DataAVCO Cost-Basis Engine16-Slide Deck

A 16-slide study of who actually controls a stock, built from the daily broker summary data the exchange publishes. Barito Pacific covers 2,530 trading days and 88 brokers. At the centre is an AVCO cost-basis engine using price-correlated lot matching, because standard cost-basis methods break down on aggregate exchange data where the seller is never the same account as the buyer.

Inside the study

  • Ownership reconciled to 100% of shares outstanding across four tiers: strategic blocks, named sub-5% holders, deduplicated broker float, and an untracked residual of 1.36% here (four of the five studies reconcile with nothing left over)
  • Market makers are scored rather than asserted, on three weighted signals: gross flow value, daily presence, and correlation between a broker’s buying and the next day’s price. CC, YP and PD clear the threshold for this stock
  • Retail-app aggregators are excluded from that scoring and labelled, since their volume is real but uncoordinated
  • A separate insider screen flags quiet accumulators by net-to-gross ratio: brokers who keep what they buy rather than churning it
  • Full broker P&L at Rp 1,752: +Rp6.1T realized, +Rp12.5T combined, 84 of 88 brokers profitable
  • Conviction bell curves by standard deviation of buy price, a 30-day against 180-day regime map, and VWAP-alpha clustering
